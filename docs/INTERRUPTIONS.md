# Historical Incidents And Interruptions

Optional `race_dynamics.history` now accepts complete earlier race logs. Each record needs event ID, season/round, circuit, race format, observation/availability times, source, completeness flag, entrants and episodes. Episodes contain kind (`incident`, `safety_car`, `virtual_safety_car`, `red_flag`), start and duration as fractions of race distance, and known affected driver IDs. Include empty episode lists for verified event-free races. Incomplete logs do not count as negative examples.

At least eight complete earlier races of the same format are required. Grand Prix and sprint records are never pooled. Same-circuit evidence is shrunk toward the format-wide empirical distribution with eight races of pooling strength. Target weekends, future feedback and duplicate weekend records are rejected. Existing observations are not automatically labelled complete: raw messages alone do not establish event-free coverage.

Simulation samples complete races with replacement to preserve observed event co-occurrence, repeated episodes and timing. Historical driver incident involvement is smoothed by a Beta(1,3) prior to allocate the sampled number of affected cars in the current field. New drivers receive the prior. Incident delay defaults to ten seconds and is a non-retirement delay; the existing DNF model stays unchanged to avoid double-counting.

Gap effects use an explicit score-to-seconds scale (default 20). Safety cars and red flags compress the accumulated part of projected gaps; VSC retains gaps. Pit opportunities depend on supplied probability and team execution, allow at most one discounted stop per car, and use green/neutralized losses (defaults 22/12 seconds). Red flags do not award random cheap-stop gains. Restarts introduce bounded-time-weighted score noise only before the finish. Overlapping compression periods are not independently stacked.

This is an experimental score-space approximation, not a full lap-by-lap physics/strategy simulator. The time scale, gap compression, pit choices, incident delays and restart variance are assumptions, not learned telemetry. It has no weather-conditioned incident rates and cannot predict a particular crash. No automatic wet-weather uplift or extra DNF risk is added. Future work must evaluate these effects chronologically before claiming accuracy or calibration improvements.

Forecast JSON includes `interruption_analysis` with sample counts, estimated probabilities and limitations. Reports display the estimates and sampled scenario event rates. With missing or insufficient history, existing manual dynamics or generic disruption behavior is retained. No existing historical datasets or saved reports were relabelled or replaced by this change.
