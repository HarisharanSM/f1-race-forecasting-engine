How to read this report
  • Forecasted position: drivers are ranked by their average simulated finishing position. It is a full ordering, not a guaranteed outcome.
  • Confidence: the share of simulations where that driver finished in the displayed position. For example, 16% at P1 means 160 wins in 1,000 simulations. For a scenario tab, this is conditional on that scenario.
  • Scenario chance: how much weight that weather scenario has in the combined forecast. This differs from a driver's confidence. The weather and disruption probabilities still contain heuristic assumptions.
  • 80% position range: the model's 10th–90th percentile finishing positions. These discrete ranges and probabilities are not calibrated guarantees.
  • Actual position: the archived classification. The displayed position difference compares the ranked forecast with actual position; the MAE score uses the unrounded average simulated position.
  • Feedback: actual order teaches pace only for drivers without known disqualifying incidents; race non-finishes teach retirement risk. Observed weather conditions training examples. Validation selects an epoch; it does not directly fit weights.
  • Whole-weekend separation: each tested weekend is excluded from its own model fitting and validation. Sprint and Grand Prix models are trained separately. Earlier sprint results do not train the Grand Prix model on the same weekend.
  • Real, reconstructed history: outcomes and weather come from archives. Roster/grid availability is reconstructed, pace uses previous-result proxies, and unknown car traits retain neutral priors. Later archive corrections and excluded sessions limit accuracy claims.
  • The final saved model uses later feedback after the test predictions were fixed. Its in-sample results are not presented as a new backtest.
